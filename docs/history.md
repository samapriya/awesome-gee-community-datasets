# Building Data Commons

**Communities are what communities build together.** Google Earth Engine (GEE) is powerful not only because of what it can compute, but because of who gathers around it — a community that iterates in the open, shares its code, and learns in public. The Awesome GEE Community Catalog began as an attempt to give that spirit a home for *data*: a place where anyone could contribute a dataset and everyone could find one.

## How it started

The catalog started with a single question. A user asked where to find Facebook's high-resolution population density maps — a dataset the official GEE catalog didn't carry. Hosted by Columbia University and resolving population down to 30 meters, it was exactly the kind of valuable, hard-to-reach layer that fell through the cracks. Rather than treat that gap as a dead end, we treated it as an experiment: what if the community itself maintained the datasets it needed?

That Facebook dataset became the first entry in the catalog — and, fittingly, one of its most frequently updated. The full origin story is captured in the foundational [blog post](https://samapriyaroy.medium.com/community-datasets-in-google-earth-engine-an-experiment-b72daa474819).

## The guiding principle

The catalog is built on an old idea about shared resources. Elinor Ostrom's Nobel-winning work on the governance of commons showed that communities can steward shared resources sustainably when they agree on the norms for using them — the same principle that carried open-source projects like Linux and collaborative knowledge bases like Wikipedia. What works for a physical commons, we believed, could work for a digital one.

So we set out to build a **Community Data Commons**: the **Awesome GEE Community Catalog**.

## Why it matters

Earth Engine democratized access to planetary-scale *processing*. The catalog aims to do the same for the *data* — lowering the barrier to a growing, curated collection of public datasets so that finding the right layer is no longer the hard part of a project.

The real challenge, and the reason a commons model fits, is scale in both directions: the catalog has to serve continental datasets and one-off community contributions with equal care. It works because the Earth Engine ecosystem already runs on learning, adaptation, and iteration. The catalog simply gives that culture a shared shelf — a bridge between the people who publish data and the people who need it.

It is, and will remain, a collaborative effort. Its continued growth depends on the community that builds it. **[Contribute a dataset](contributing/submit.md)** and help the commons grow.
